Engineer’s pain

Made of pain.

I’ve worked in CAD for years, and for years something in it drove me mad. Here are the most painful bits and how BreezeCAD solves them.

— Mikhail Verkholashin, author of BreezeCAD

01Controls adapt to you 02Threads that actually print 03No windows over the model 04Your work won’t vanish 05Parts hold on to each other
pain 01

Whose mouse is this?

During the day I have three or four programs open, and each has its own opinion about the mouse. In one, holding the wheel pans the part; in another the same move rotates it. The wheel zooms in here and out there. By lunch my hand confidently spins the part the wrong way.

fix

Controls adapt to you.

  • Ready-made mouse presets matching the habits of popular programs: pick one and your hands work the way they’re used to.
  • Or your own: rotate, pan and zoom on any button with Shift, Ctrl or Alt, wheel direction, rotation speed and style.
  • Keyboard shortcuts — presets or anything you like. Your set saves to a file and moves with you to another computer.
  • A separate tab for the touchpad: two fingers with modifiers, pinch, rotate.

Hands remember — the program adapts.

pain 02

Threads that exist only in the picture

You add a thread, send it to the slicer — and get a smooth cylinder. The thread was cosmetic, just a picture on the face. Want to print it? Model the turns by hand and try to remember the pitch of M8.

fix

Threads that actually print.

  • Click a shaft or a hole and the size is detected. A 6.8 mm hole is recognised as a tap hole for M8.
  • Coarse ISO pitch by default; type a fine one by hand if you need it.
  • The turns are real ISO 60° geometry and go straight into STL and 3MF.
  • Heavy model? Show threads as cosmetic — the export still gets the real ones.

One click — the rest is up to your printer.

pain 03

The window hides what you wanted to click

You start measuring and the result window lands exactly on the edge you need to click. You build a plane through three points, miss the third — and the whole selection resets. Start over.

fix

No windows over the model.

  • Measurement results live in the left panel: distance, ΔX/ΔY/ΔZ, areas, radii and a Copy button. The model shows only a line and a label.
  • Every selected item has its own field with a cross: you can clear one point, not everything at once.
  • A missed click resets nothing.

Count how many times a day you move windows around.

pain 04

Ctrl+S after every operation

Every designer has the reflex: one operation — Ctrl+S. Nobody invented it; it was trained into us. Usually at the cost of hours of work that vanished along with the program window.

fix

Your work won’t vanish.

  • Autosave every 2, 5 or 10 minutes — even in the middle of a sketch.
  • The copy is written while you pause, so nothing stutters.
  • The last three copies of every document are kept.
  • After a crash the home page shows “Unsaved work”: one button and you’re back where you stopped.
  • And if the program does crash, you get a window with an error code you can copy and send.

Ctrl+S is now a habit, not insurance.

pain 05

One fillet, half the assembly in pieces

I’m making an enclosure for a board: I build the box right off the base, project the outline, 0.2 mm gap — lovely. Then I notice sharp edges and add a 1 mm fillet. I open the box and the sketch is red: the projections lost their edges, and the extrusion and the mates went down with them. A one-millimetre fillet, then an hour of archaeology: what was built from what, and how to glue it back. Good thing Ctrl+S was a reflex by then.

fix · 0.2.5.1

Parts hold on to each other.

  • A projection now relies not on the body edge but on the sketch line that edge grew from. A fillet, chamfer or shell leaves a line a line: it may get shorter, but the reference holds.
  • A projection is lost only if its source line is deleted. Older files pick this up on their own when opened.
  • Changed the sketch itself — added a contour, moved a dimension? When you finish editing, BreezeCAD shows “A change affecting other parts was detected”, one part at a time.
  • Four answers: keep it as is, adapt automatically, follow the new contour at a gap (mimicking) or open the sketches for manual editing — with constraints rolled back to how they were before the break.
  • On the right, a preview: the changed volume glows with a red haze visible from any angle. Each decision is a single undo step.

Fillet away — the box won’t take offence.
